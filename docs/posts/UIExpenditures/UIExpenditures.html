Attempt 1: Simply Analyzing Total Requests

The first attempt simply looked at the total requests each of the professors made over the relevant time period. The Principal Investigator (PI) and Co-Investigator (CoI) totals are summed with each other, and the Co-Principal Investigator (Co-PI) totals are plotted separately. This ran into several issues - first, this does not display how each faculty member’s requests change over time, and second, the graph is unsorted.

PIData = data.loc[data['ROLE'] == 'PI']

combined = make_subplots(

To make the heat map effective, the requests data needs to be rearranged. To show the total amount each professor has requested cumulatively over time, each year’s data will need to be composed of not just that year’s data, but the cumulative amount of all previous years. This is explained in the below code block as follows:

Since the first year of requests is in 2019, Year 1 is just 2019’s data. The total requests after Year 2 will then be 2020’s data plus 2019’s data. Year 3’s total requests will therefore be the combined total of 2019, 2020 and 2021, and so on. The data will then need to be altered a little bit, since for some professors, they didn’t start until well after 2019 - so their “Year 1” can be different from others. To fix this, the data will be shifted left, so that all “year 1”s are in the same column. For those that started later than 2019, the later years will be in the future for them. This will be presented as null values, and will show up as blank tiles on the visualizations to come.

This first heat map will consist of the cumulative amount requested each professor has gotten over time since they started requesting. This attempt, shown below, showcases a glaring problem with this method at first - the outliers.
